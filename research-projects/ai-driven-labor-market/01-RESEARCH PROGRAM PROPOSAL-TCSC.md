## **1\. Executive Summary**

Artificial intelligence, data technologies and digital transformation are changing occupations and the skills required in the labor market. These changes affect not only computer science students, but also students in engineering, business, finance, healthcare, law, communication and other fields.

Many students are uncertain about the careers that may grow in the future, the skills they should develop and how they should organize their learning during university. This research program will examine future-oriented occupations, essential skills and practical learning pathways for students from different academic backgrounds.

The research will rely mainly on secondary data, publicly available reports, open datasets and academic literature. It will use sources such as the World Economic Forum, the World Bank, the OECD and the International Labour Organization.

The long-term goal is not to predict the future with certainty or recommend one career to every student. It is to develop an evidence-based framework that helps students understand how AI may affect their field and how they can progressively develop relevant technical, digital, analytical and human skills.

## **2\. Background and Problem Statement**

Artificial intelligence is transforming the way organizations operate and changing the tasks performed in many occupations. It is affecting sectors such as technology, finance, healthcare, agriculture, education, law, communication and administration.

International reports identify skills such as artificial intelligence, big data, cybersecurity, technological literacy, analytical thinking, creativity, communication, adaptability and lifelong learning as increasingly important.weforum+1

However, many university students do not know:

* which careers may grow in the future;  
* how AI may affect their field of study;  
* which skills employers increasingly value;  
* how to organize their learning;  
* how to combine academic knowledge with practical skills.

This creates uncertainty about career decisions and professional preparation. The problem is not limited to computer science. Students from different academic fields need different combinations of technical, digital and human skills.

Therefore, this research will examine how students can prepare for an AI-driven labor market while recognizing the differences between academic fields.

## **3\. Research Question and Objectives**

## **Main Research Question**

> **What skills and learning pathways can help university students from different academic fields prepare for the AI-driven labor market?**

## **Supporting Questions**

1. Which occupations and sectors are expected to be influenced by artificial intelligence?  
2. Which technical, digital, analytical and human skills are becoming more important?  
3. Which skills are common across different academic fields?  
4. How can students progressively organize their learning?  
5. What are the limitations of predicting future careers and skills?

## **Main Objective**

To examine future careers and skills in an AI-driven labor market and propose flexible learning pathways for university students from different academic fields.

## **Specific Objectives**

* Analyze the skills mentioned in international labor-market reports.  
* Compare common and field-specific skills.  
* Propose a progressive learning framework for students.

## **4\. Methodology and Data Sources**

## **Research Design (Methodology)**

The study will use a **secondary mixed-methods research design**.

It will combine:

* analysis of international reports;  
* analysis of publicly available datasets;  
* descriptive statistics;  
* comparative analysis;  
* qualitative document analysis;  
* classification of occupations and skills.

No primary data collection is planned for the first study.

## **Main Sources**

**World Economic Forum — Future of Jobs Report 2025**

This report identifies emerging and declining occupations and examines the skills expected to become more important in the coming years.[weforum](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/)

**World Bank — Digital Progress and Trends Report 2025**

This report examines digital skills, artificial intelligence skills, generative AI skills and differences between economies.openknowledge.worldbank+1

**OECD — Artificial Intelligence and the Changing Demand for Skills**

The OECD analyzes how AI changes work tasks and skill requirements. It also emphasizes that most workers exposed to AI will not necessarily need specialized machine-learning skills.oecd+1

**International Labour Organization**

ILO reports may provide additional information about employment, automation and the future of work.

## **Planned Analysis**

The research will:

1. identify occupations and skills mentioned across the selected sources;  
2. classify skills into technical, digital, analytical, human and adaptive categories;  
3. compare common and field-specific skills;  
4. distinguish observed trends from projections;  
5. develop a flexible learning framework for students.

## **5\. Proposed Research Roadmap and Expected Contribution**

The research program will develop progressively through several possible studies.

## **Study 1: Future Occupations and Skills**

> **Which occupations and skills are repeatedly identified as important for the future of work?**

This first study will compare international reports and identify the most frequently mentioned occupations and competencies.

## **Study 2: Skills Across Academic Fields**

> **Which skills are common across academic fields, and which are specific to particular careers?**

This study will compare fields such as computer science, engineering, business, healthcare, law and communication.

## **Study 3: Learning Pathways for Students**

> **How can students organize their learning to prepare for an AI-driven labor market?**

This study will propose a progression such as:

> **Foundation → Exploration → Specialization → Practical Application → Lifelong Learning**

## **Expected Contribution**

The research is expected to produce:

* a classification of future-oriented occupations;  
* a classification of essential technical and non-technical skills;  
* a comparison of skills across academic fields;  
* a flexible learning framework for students;  
* recommendations based on international evidence;  
* a foundation for future research on data-driven career guidance.

The goal is not to prescribe the same learning path for all students. Each student should combine common skills with specialized skills related to their chosen field.

## **6\. Scope and Limitations** 

## **Scope**

The study will focus on:

* university students from different academic fields;  
* AI and the future of work;  
* emerging occupations;  
* digital, technical, analytical and human skills;  
* learning pathways;  
* global labor-market trends.

The study will not attempt to:

* predict jobs with complete certainty;  
* measure the skills of individual students;  
* analyze every job advertisement worldwide;  
* develop an AI career recommendation system in the first stage.

## **Limitations**

* Future labor-market projections are not guarantees.  
* Online job postings do not represent every job or country.  
* Some detailed labor-market datasets may not be completely open.  
* Sources may define skills and occupations differently.  
* Global trends may not apply equally to every local labor market.  
* The first study will rely on secondary data rather than direct student feedback.
