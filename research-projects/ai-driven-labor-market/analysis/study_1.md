***Which occupations and skills are repeatedly identified as important for the future of work?**

### 1\. Key Occupations Growing in Importance

The sources distinguish between occupations experiencing the **fastest percentage growth** (predominantly tech- and green-driven) and those driving the **largest absolute job volume** (essential human-centered services, care, and frontline work)[1]:

* **Technology, Data, and AI Specialists:**
  * Ten of the 15 fastest-growing jobs globally are in technology or technology-enabled roles[2].
  * Specific roles cited with the highest projected growth rates include **AI and Machine Learning Specialists**, **Big Data Specialists**, **Data Analysts and Scientists**, **Software and Applications Developers**, **Fintech Engineers**, **Database/Data Warehousing Specialists**, **DevOps Engineers**, and **Information Security/Cybersecurity Analysts**[1].
* **Green Transition and Sustainability Roles:**
  * Driven by climate mitigation, decarbonization, and renewable energy adoption, jobs such as **Renewable Energy Engineers**, **Environmental Engineers**, **Sustainability Specialists**, and **Autonomous and Electric Vehicle Specialists** rank among the fastest-growing occupations worldwide[1].
* **Care Economy and Healthcare Occupations:**
  * Demographic shifts, especially aging populations in high-income economies, are fueling widespread demand for **Nursing Professionals**, **Personal Care Aides**, and **Social Work and Counselling Professionals**[1].
* **Education and Training Roles:**
  * Expanding working-age populations in emerging economies and broad reskilling needs across industries are driving net job creation for **University and Higher Education Teachers**, **Secondary Education Teachers**, and **Vocational Education Teachers**[1].
* **High-Volume Frontline and Operational Roles:**
  * In absolute employment numbers, roles such as **Light Truck and Delivery Drivers**, **Building Construction Workers**, **Farmworkers and Agricultural Workers**, and **Food Processing Workers** continue to see some of the largest net additions[1].

*(In contrast, the occupations repeatedly forecast to decline most sharply due to automation and digital workflows are* **clerical and secretarial roles** *, including Data Entry Clerks, Administrative Assistants, Bank Tellers, Cashiers, and Accounting/Bookkeeping Clerks*[1]*).*

### 2\. Core and Rising Skills for the Future

The reports emphasize that technical proficiency alone is insufficient; workers require a **triad of foundational, technical, and transversal (human) capabilities**[16]:

#### A. Cognitive and Analytical Skills

* **Analytical Thinking:** Consistently identified by employers as the **number-one core skill** required across industries (deemed essential by 70% of surveyed firms)[19][20].
* **Creative Thinking and Originality:** Highly prized for complex problem solving, innovation, and tasks requiring non-standard idea generation[19].
* **Systems Thinking and Computational Thinking:** Needed to understand complex interdependencies, break down unstructured challenges, and manage interactions with automated and autonomous systems[23].

#### B. Self-Efficacy and Adaptability

* **Resilience, Flexibility, and Agility:** Ranks as the **second-most essential core skill** and the single strongest differentiator between growing and declining job roles[19].
* **Curiosity and Lifelong Learning:** With an estimated 39% of core worker skill sets expected to transform by 2030, continuous learning and adaptability are critical to prevent skill obsolescence[19].
* **Motivation and Self-Awareness:** Essential for autonomy, personal discipline, and navigating changing workflows[20].

#### C. Technology and Digital Literacy

* **AI and Big Data:** Consistently tops employer lists as the **fastest-growing skill in demand**[19].
* **Technological Literacy &amp; Networks/Cybersecurity:** Ranked among the top three fastest-rising competencies, reflecting the universal digitalization of workplaces and the need to protect data and infrastructure[19].
* **Intermediate &amp; Practical Digital Skills:** Beyond specialized AI developers, everyday workers increasingly require competencies to embed generative AI, digital tools, and business software into routine workflows[34].

#### D. Human, Social, and Management Skills

* **Leadership and Social Influence:** Essential for guiding teams through organizational transformation and crisis[19].
* **Collaboration and Team Coordination:** Skills emphasizing teamwork, negotiation, and cross-functional cooperation have seen large increases in demand across high-exposure occupations[18].
* **Empathy and Active Listening:** Strongly demanded in care, customer-facing, and talent-retention environments[20].
* **Talent Management and Mentoring:** Rapidly rising in priority as organizations navigate demographic shifts, workforce reskilling, and talent scarcity[7].
* **Environmental Stewardship:** Emerging for the first time in global top-10 rising skills lists due to sustainability requirements and green regulations[7].

### The Central Takeaway

The common thread across all reports is the **augmentation principle**: while AI and automation replace routine cognitive and clerical tasks, they amplify the value of **durable, non-automatable human skills** (analytical reasoning, creative problem-solving, empathy, adaptability, and ethical judgment) combined with **practical digital fluency**
